# GTM Playbooks by Business Archetype

Select one primary archetype. If the model combines software, service, marketplace, or regulated intermediation, state which motion controls the hardest constraint.

## Comparison

| Archetype | Earliest credible test | First-customer motion | Main proof | Common scale blocker |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B2B SaaS | Problem interviews plus manual workflow, design partnership, or paid pilot | Founder-led sales to a narrow ICP | Business outcome, champion reference, integration/security feasibility | Hiring sales before the founder can teach a repeatable motion |
| B2C SaaS | Single-job prototype with payment and cohort behavior | One concentrated intent, community, product, or creator channel | Activation, retained use, paid conversion, referral | Buying installs before retention and contribution economics work |
| B2C fintech/insurtech | Safe prototype or advisor-assisted concierge with regulatory review | Trust-led direct cohort or regulated/affinity distribution partner | Comprehension, trust, licensed delivery, retained funded/insured behavior | Compliance, fraud/claims, support, weak trust, and cross-subsidized CAC |
| B2C professional services | Paid concierge delivery for one trigger and package | Trigger-based outreach, communities, referrals, and trusted local partners | Reviews, completed outcome, time-to-value, repeat/referral | Founder capacity, inconsistent delivery, geographic sprawl, low contribution margin |

## B2B SaaS

### Test

- Select an ICP using workflow, trigger, buyer, current tool, urgency, and reachable account list.
- Interview recent problem owners, then manually deliver the outcome or layer a narrow feature on an existing workflow.
- Seek a meaningful commitment: data access, implementation time, letter of intent, or preferably a paid pilot.
- Treat the first roughly five customers as an education cohort. Document which requests are shared versus account-specific.

### Launch

- Founder owns discovery, demos, objection learning, closing, and early success.
- Start with warm domain access, trigger-based outbound, professional associations, niche events, or incumbent/integration ecosystems.
- Build proof in this order: customer language, workflow demo, quantified pilot result, reference, security/integration answer.
- Use content to support direct sales before expecting content to create a predictable pipeline.

### Repeat and scale

- Standardize qualification, demo, pilot, implementation, and customer-success steps.
- Hire sales only when the founder can define ICP, qualification, common objections, expected cycle, win/loss reasons, and a teachable playbook.
- Expand marketing after the first customers provide credible proof; empirical European research supports personal sales early and broader media later.
- Add integration, reseller, association, accountant/consultant, or platform partnerships only after a manual referral/co-sell test shows qualified access.

### Failure patterns

- Broad “SMB” targeting, free pilots without conversion dates, custom-roadmap capture, outsourced selling before learning, channel partners with no active pipeline, and marketing-qualified leads that never reach a real buyer.

## B2C SaaS

### Test

- Reduce the product to one repeated job and one activation moment.
- Recruit users from one concentrated context where the job already occurs.
- Test price or commitment early; a waitlist is not equivalent to payment or retained use.
- Measure cohorts from acquisition source through activation, retained value, cancellation, and referral.

### Launch

- Choose one primary discovery loop: explicit search/app-store intent, niche community, creator/expert audience, useful shareable artifact, or product invite.
- Align the promise to the customer's desired outcome, not feature volume. In the Popsa case, a concrete time-to-value promise materially improved app-store conversion before paid scale.
- Make onboarding deliver the promised value quickly and instrument the drop-off points.
- Use lifecycle communication as part of the product experience, not a substitute for product value.

### Repeat and scale

- Scale a channel only when its cohort activates and retains acceptably and its contribution economics are plausible.
- Find the constraint in the customer journey and run focused experiments rather than copying a generic growth checklist.
- Add bundles, affiliates, platforms, employers, creators, or device/channel partners when they own a relevant audience and the value exchange is measurable.
- Separate acquisition spikes from durable growth; high downloads with weak retention are not product-market fit.

### Failure patterns

- Paying for installs before retention, relying on notification pressure, optimizing trial starts instead of retained value, discount-driven subscribers who churn, and expanding to many languages before the home-market funnel works.

## B2C Fintech and Insurtech

### Test

- Separate the consumer, payer, regulated principal, licensed advisor/broker/carrier/bank, and data owner.
- Start with education, comparison, simulation, or human-reviewed concierge using synthetic/minimized data where possible.
- Verify licensing, financial promotion, suitability/advice, disclosures, consent, AML/KYC, data, complaints, and claims responsibilities before live distribution.
- Test trust and comprehension as well as conversion. A fast signup that creates unsuitable or misunderstood outcomes is a failed test.

### Launch

- Lead with one high-frequency or high-anxiety job, transparent scope, responsive support, and visible trust proof.
- Consider a regulated partner, employer/benefit platform, broker/advisor, bank, association, retailer, or affinity group when it controls trust or the trigger event.
- Run partner referrals manually before API integration. Agree eligibility, consent, handoff, service levels, complaints, economics, and attribution.
- Use referral incentives carefully; measure funded, active, retained, or appropriately covered customers—not signups alone.

### Repeat and scale

- Scale only after compliance operations, fraud/claims, customer support, retention, and unit economics survive a real cohort.
- Use regulatory innovation hubs or sandboxes as scoped learning mechanisms, not as substitutes for authorization.
- Expand country by country in Europe because product legality, language, disclosures, intermediaries, and trust channels differ.
- Add products only when the first wedge has earned trust and produces a credible cross-sell moment.

### Failure patterns

- Growth incentives that attract low-quality accounts, treating a license as GTM, hiding revenue-share economics, unreviewed automated advice, expansion before local compliance/support readiness, and subsidized growth mistaken for demand.

## B2C Professional Services

Examples include relocation, immigration coordination, travel planning, local concierge, education placement, and other expert-assisted outcomes.

### Test

- Pick one trigger, destination/use case, customer profile, and bounded package.
- Deliver manually to a small paid cohort; require a deposit or other meaningful commitment.
- Track founder hours, third-party cost, exception rate, completion time, customer anxiety, refund/rework, and contribution margin.
- Convert delivery learning into a checklist, exclusions, evidence requirements, service level, and escalation path.

### Launch

- Acquire at the trigger: expat/travel communities, employers, universities, agents, accommodation providers, airlines, mobility providers, local experts, or high-intent search.
- Build trust with transparent process, named experts, response times, reviews, sample deliverables, and clear boundaries.
- Use partnerships when the partner sees the customer before or during the trigger. Start with referral codes, co-hosted education, or manual handoffs.
- Concentrate geographically until service quality, partner coverage, and review density are strong.

### Repeat and scale

- Productize repeated work into packages and internal tooling without removing judgment from exceptional cases.
- Hire or contract delivery capacity only after quality standards and unit economics are observable.
- Expand one geography or trigger at a time; rebuild the provider, regulatory, content, support, and partnership layer locally.
- For marketplace-like models, validate supply quality and demand separately; GetYourGuide's early supplier pull helped reveal a better professional-supply wedge.

### Failure patterns

- Free bespoke advice, undefined scope, scaling leads beyond delivery capacity, national/global coverage before local density, affiliate partnerships without quality control, and automation that damages trust.
