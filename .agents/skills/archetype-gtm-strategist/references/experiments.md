# GTM Experiment and Decision-Gate Library

Use ranges as planning defaults, not universal truth. Set thresholds before running the test and adjust them for price, cycle length, risk, and baseline data.

## Evidence ladder

| Stage | Question | Stronger evidence | Do not confuse with evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Is the job painful and triggered now? | Recent behavior, workaround, cost/risk, failed attempts | Opinions, broad interest, trend data |
| Offer | Will the segment commit? | Deposit, payment, data/time access, signed pilot/LOI with next date | Compliments, waitlist, unqualified demo request |
| Value | Does the MVP deliver the outcome? | Activation and outcome completion | Feature usage without outcome |
| Retention | Does value repeat? | Cohort retention, renewal, repeat purchase, continued funded/covered state | Downloads, first-session activity |
| Distribution | Can one motion repeat? | Similar customers from the same channel at plausible cost | One founder relationship, PR spike |
| Scale | Can economics and operations withstand volume? | Contribution margin, payback, support/claims/fraud capacity, stable quality | Revenue growth with hidden subsidy |

## Core test cards

### Trigger/problem interviews

- Recruit roughly 8–15 people with a recent trigger, not convenient acquaintances.
- Ask about the last event, workaround, spend, approval, search and decision path.
- Pass when a narrow segment repeatedly describes the same costly job and reachable context; otherwise narrow or stop.

### Concierge or manual service

- Deliver the outcome manually to 3–5 paying or meaningfully committed customers.
- Record every step, exception, hour, external cost, anxiety point, and result.
- Pass when customers complete the process and the service can plausibly achieve positive contribution after realistic labor.

### B2B design-partner or paid-pilot test

- Target 10–20 qualified accounts to obtain a small number of committed design partners or paid pilots.
- Define scope, success metric, data/security responsibility, conversion date, price, and non-goals.
- Pass only when the same job and buying logic repeat and at least some customers convert or commit on commercial terms.

### B2C activation/retention test

- Recruit a source-identifiable cohort large enough to observe the main drop-offs; 20–50 target users is often a practical early start.
- Define the activation event and expected repeat interval before launch.
- Pass when the intended segment reaches value and returns or pays at a rate that supports further testing; do not invent a universal retention benchmark.

### Message/offer smoke test

- Test 2–3 problem/offer variants against the same audience and conversion action.
- Use payment, booked qualified call, deposit, or completed application where ethical; disclose limitations.
- Treat clicks and email capture as directional only.

### Founder-led outbound test

- Build a tightly qualified account list from explicit trigger or workflow evidence.
- Run small batches with personalized problem language, then revise from replies and calls.
- Track positive replies, qualified conversations, opportunities, wins, cycle, and reasons lost—not send volume.

### Partnership pilot

- Interview 5–10 candidate partners about audience overlap, trigger access, incentives, workflow, risk, and economics.
- Select one or two for a manual referral/co-sell test before integration.
- Pass when qualified introductions recur, handoffs work, both sides see value, attribution is credible, and economics survive support/revenue share.

### Paid channel test

- Run only after activation and retention/repeat behavior are measurable.
- Cap spend and define customer-quality, conversion, contribution, and payback thresholds in advance.
- Compare channel cohorts; stop channels that buy low-quality activation or churn.

### Country expansion test

- Verify local problem language, price, alternatives, legal path, support, payment, partner coverage, and acquisition costs.
- Run one segment/city/channel wedge with local proof.
- Scale only when the localized cohort and delivery economics approach the home-market standard or justify a deliberate difference.

## Metrics by archetype

### B2B SaaS

Qualified reply and meeting rates, opportunity conversion, paid-pilot conversion, win rate, sales cycle, ACV, implementation completion, activation, retained usage, expansion, gross margin, CAC/payback.

### B2C SaaS

Source-qualified acquisition, activation, time-to-value, paid conversion, cohort retention, cancellation/refund, referral, ARPU/LTV assumptions, contribution margin, CAC/payback.

### B2C fintech/insurtech

Qualified/funded/covered activation, comprehension, suitability or advice review, KYC completion, support and complaint rate, fraud/claims behavior, retained balances/policies/use, partner conversion, net revenue, contribution, CAC/payback.

### B2C professional services

Qualified inquiry, deposit/payment, completion, time-to-outcome, delivery hours, exception/rework/refund, review/referral, partner conversion, contribution per case, capacity utilization.

## Scale gate

Do not scale unless the team can answer:

1. Who converts and retains best, and after which trigger?
2. What exact outcome activates value?
3. Which channel produces those customers repeatedly?
4. What does the full channel and delivery cost include?
5. What breaks operationally or regulatorily at 5–10× volume?
6. Can another trained person execute the motion without founder improvisation?
