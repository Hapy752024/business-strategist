# Regional GTM Adaptations

Apply region only after choosing the archetype and stage. These are starting hypotheses, not substitutes for country-specific evidence.

## Europe — priority 1

### Default stance

- Choose one country, language, trigger, and trust context as the beachhead. “Europe” is not one launch market.
- Validate local willingness to pay, category language, consumer expectations, contracting, tax, privacy, licensing, and support before translating campaigns.
- Use local professional bodies, employers, banks/carriers, associations, travel/mobility players, and trusted communities where they control access or credibility.
- Build country-specific proof: local references, relevant certifications/authorizations, local service coverage, and response standards.
- Treat cross-border expansion as a new GTM test with reused capabilities—not a copy/paste campaign.

### Stage implications

- Early B2B: founder sales and local references close the trust gap; broader marketing follows proof.
- Consumer SaaS: language and payment localization matter, but do not fragment product and marketing before one cohort retains.
- Fintech/insurtech: identify the national competent authority and relevant innovation hub early. EIOPA maintains an EEA map of hubs and sandboxes, including cross-border testing facilities.
- Professional services: local partners and delivery capacity are part of the product; partner quality and geographic density precede broad lead generation.

### Europe-to-US expansion

- Revalidate segment language, price, competitive alternatives, procurement or state rules, support hours, and channel costs.
- Use a narrow customer or partner beachhead rather than opening a general US campaign.
- Do not mistake English-language readiness for operational readiness.

## United States — priority 2

### Default stance

- A larger shared-language market can support narrower vertical ICPs and faster channel experiments, but competition and paid acquisition can be expensive.
- Founder-led outbound, vertical communities, integrations, app ecosystems, high-intent search, and creator/expert distribution remain stage-dependent.
- State-level licensing, insurance, travel-seller, privacy, tax, and professional-service rules can fragment a seemingly national launch.
- Treat accelerators, Product Hunt, press, and launch events as awareness tests, not validation.

### Transfer rules

- Import the European product or evidence advantage, not the European message verbatim.
- Secure US references and test price expectations before staffing a sales or marketing team.
- For fintech/insurtech, determine sponsor bank/carrier/licensed-entity economics and control responsibilities before acquisition scale.

## China — priority 3, lower-confidence layer

### Default stance

- Start with a locally specific problem, audience, platform behavior, language, and regulatory path. Do not translate a Western funnel and call it localization.
- Test platform-native discovery and trust: useful content, community participation, KOC/KOL or expert proof, search behavior, and social commerce where appropriate.
- Treat WeChat/private-domain retention, Xiaohongshu discovery, Douyin content/commerce, mini-programs, and marketplaces as distinct hypotheses with different user intent.
- Use a local operator or partner for platform operations, payments, customer support, data, licensing, and rapidly changing promotion rules.
- Protect against single-platform dependency by capturing consented customer relationships where lawful and by measuring retained value outside campaign vanity metrics.

### Evidence pattern

Xiaohongshu began with a shopping guide assembled from local shopping experts, then expanded into user-generated reviews/community, and only later into direct commerce with licensed brands. The transferable mechanism is content/community learning before transaction expansion—not copying the platform or assuming the same channel will work.

### Transfer rules

- Require current Chinese-language source checks and local expert review before material spend or regulated launch.
- Test one city/audience/platform wedge first.
- Measure qualified conversations, activated users, repeat behavior, and contribution economics; views, saves, and follower growth remain weak without downstream behavior.
- For fintech/insurtech, do not proceed from general GTM advice; obtain jurisdiction- and product-specific legal/regulatory review and a credible licensed partner path.
