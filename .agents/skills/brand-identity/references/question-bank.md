# Discovery Question Bank

Use one question at a time.

When giving answer suggestions, format them as a numbered list and invite the user to reply with the number.

## Goal

- What is the main business or communication problem this identity must solve?
- Is this a new brand, a refresh, or a systematization of existing assets?

## Deliverables

- Which outputs matter most first: guidelines, logo, colors, UI kit, marketing assets, or templates?
- Is this for digital-only use, print use, or both?

## Audience

- Which target customer segment and country/region should this brand primarily serve?
- Is the brand local, national, regional, or global?
- Who must trust, buy from, use, or join this organization?
- What should this audience feel within the first five seconds?

Suggested format:

1. B2B enterprise customers in DACH.
2. Small businesses in the US.
3. Consumers in Switzerland.
4. Public-sector organizations in the EU.

## Differentiation

- What makes the company or brand meaningfully different from competitors?
- Which one promise should the audience remember first?

Suggested USP angles:

1. Fastest or easiest solution.
2. Most secure or reliable option.
3. Highest craft or premium quality.
4. Most local, personal, or human service.
5. Most innovative or technically advanced.

## Design Direction Preferences

Ask only when helpful, one question at a time.

Logo concept:

1. Abstract symbol.
2. Literal metaphor.
3. Wordmark.
4. Technical mark.

Color strategy:

1. Follow category expectations.
2. Contrast deliberately from competitors.
3. Blend category trust cues with one distinctive accent.

Typography:

1. Premium/editorial.
2. Utilitarian/technical.
3. Friendly/human.
4. Bold/disruptive.

Messaging hierarchy:

1. Lead with USP.
2. Lead with audience problem.
3. Lead with product/service outcome.
4. Lead with trust/proof.

## Values

- Which 3-5 principles should the brand express?
- Which traits should the brand explicitly avoid?

## Examples

- What specifically works in this example: color, typography, layout, motion, tone, imagery, or perceived quality?
- Are we borrowing a principle from this example or competing against it?
