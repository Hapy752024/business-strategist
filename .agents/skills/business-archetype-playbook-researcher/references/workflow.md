# Business Archetype Playbook Researcher Workflow

## Procedure

1. Define the archetype: B2B SaaS, B2C SaaS, marketplace, ecommerce, local service, agency/consultancy, regulated broker/intermediary, fintech, insurtech, or another explicit model.
2. Record stage, geography, buyer, founder resources, price level, sales cycle, and regulatory constraints.
3. Create `playbooks/<archetype>.md` in the active topic workspace.
4. Search in widening order: authoritative curricula/regulators; founder interviews and postmortems; YouTube transcripts; Reddit and specialist forums; podcasts/blogs with named operators.
5. Query for concrete actions: validation, first 10 customers, founder-led sales, pricing, launch, churn, failed channels, unit economics, hiring, and scale gates.
6. Capture operator identity, business context, date, outcome, tactic, prerequisite, and failure condition. Anonymous posts remain anecdotal.
7. Triangulate important tactics across at least two independent operators or one operator plus outcome evidence.
8. Separate customer-demand evidence from operator advice. A founder story never proves that the current topic has demand.
9. Compare source businesses with the user's segment, geography, price, regulation, and resources before transferring advice.
10. Rank tactics by evidence strength, cost, reversibility, and fit; include counterexamples.

## Output

- Archetype and scope.
- Source registry.
- Segmentation and beachhead patterns.
- Validation and launch sequence.
- First-ten-customer methods.
- Minimal-cost marketing and sales tactics.
- Pricing, retention, and unit-economics lessons.
- Scale gates and hiring triggers.
- Failed approaches and survivorship-bias warnings.
- Applicability matrix and next experiments.

## Quality Checklist

- Advice is tied to a named context and source.
- Anecdotes are labeled and not counted as demand proof.
- Failures and counterexamples are represented.
- Regulated archetypes include current authoritative sources.
- Recommendations fit the founder's actual resources and stage.
