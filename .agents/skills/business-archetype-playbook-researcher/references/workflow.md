# Business Archetype Playbook Researcher Workflow

## Procedure

1. Define the archetype: B2B SaaS, B2C SaaS, marketplace, ecommerce, local service, agency/consultancy, regulated broker/intermediary, fintech, insurtech, or another explicit model.
2. Record stage, geography, buyer, founder resources, price level, sales cycle, and regulatory constraints.
3. Reuse the active topic's existing playbook if current and applicable. Put new supporting narratives in `deep-dives/operator-playbook.md`, link them from README, and summarize the decision-relevant lessons there. Preserve legacy playbook paths and machine-generated run artifacts.
4. When the industry, business model and target customer are clear, search both the web and relevant social media for named founders/operators who built comparable successful businesses. Include relevant examples from other countries from the outset; international research need not wait for local examples to run out. Prefer the same industry and comparable models, and use cross-industry analogs with explicit transfer limits. Foreign success stories can inform the venture idea itself: alternative segments, offers, business models, service designs and acquisition approaches, as well as execution tactics. Include company evidence, founder interviews/blogs, YouTube interviews/transcripts, podcasts, LinkedIn/X posts, Reddit and specialist communities as appropriate. Use the source-capability lookup and applicable provider permissions; record inaccessible social coverage rather than inventing findings. Prefer cases matching the founder's scale and capital constraints, not only famous venture-funded outliers. Consult curricula or regulators when needed to assess a proposed action.
5. Query for concrete actions: validation, first 10 customers, founder-led sales, pricing, launch, churn, failed channels, unit economics, hiring, and scale gates.
6. Capture operator identity, business context, date, outcome, tactic, prerequisite, and failure condition. Anonymous posts remain anecdotal.
   Reconstruct the first 12–24 months (or the founder's specified horizon): initial segment/need, positioning, pre-existing audience/book/network, first customers, acquisition, sales, service and retention, capital and founder workload. Distinguish entrants starting from zero from established businesses changing legal form. Seek another country where the same customer job is served and a failed/contrasting attempt; explain absent evidence. Current success cannot fill missing early-period economics. Consult the conditional founder-source reference for SaaS; do not confuse launch attention with retained revenue.
   Define success in terms relevant to the decision: profitability, durable revenue, owner income, survival or another explicit outcome. Record each claim's source/date and whether independently supported, company-reported, founder-reported or unverified. Followers, funding and publicity alone do not establish a successful business; current scale does not explain how first customers were acquired.
7. Triangulate important tactics across at least two independent operators or one operator plus outcome evidence.
8. Separate customer-demand evidence from operator advice. A founder story never proves that the current topic has demand.
9. Compare source businesses with the user's segment, geography, price, regulation, and resources before transferring advice. For international cases, identify differences in customer behaviour, distribution, market structure and delivery economics. State what can transfer, what needs adaptation, and the cheapest local test. Foreign success supports an inspiration or mechanism hypothesis, not proof of local demand or a local competitor gap; classify it in the cross-market analog lane when a competitive landscape is used.
10. Rank tactics by evidence strength, cost, reversibility, and fit; include counterexamples.

For each useful pattern, explain the mechanism, evidence and alternative explanations; when it worked, what it required, what to try now, and what to avoid or defer. Seek failed attempts or contrasting cases to challenge survivor stories. A single example stays a case hypothesis, not an industry-wide pattern. Translate lessons into bounded next tests with a success/stop condition; do not copy tactics merely because a successful founder used them. Keep the study proportional to the decision and state when comparable success evidence could not be verified.

## Output

- Archetype and scope.
- Source registry.
- Segmentation and beachhead patterns.
- Validation and launch sequence.
- First-ten-customer methods.
- Minimal-cost marketing and sales tactics.
- Pricing, retention, and unit-economics lessons.
- Scale gates and hiring triggers.
- Failed approaches and survivorship-bias warnings.
- Applicability matrix and next experiments.

Use one compact lessons table where helpful: `Pattern | Cases and dated sources | Outcome evidence | Why it may work / prerequisites | To do or test | Avoid or defer / why | Fit and uncertainty`. Link named businesses to their verified websites and include the source list with dates. Summarize only lessons that affect the active decision in README.

## Quality Checklist

- Advice is tied to a named context and source.
- Anecdotes are labeled and not counted as demand proof.
- Failures and counterexamples are represented.
- Regulated archetypes include current authoritative sources.
- Recommendations fit the founder's actual resources and stage.
