# Service Decision Contexts

Choose contexts from evidence; do not use all of them automatically.

## Urgent outcome seeker

- Trigger: deadline, disruption, move, trip, claim, or immediate operational problem.
- Optimizes: availability, certainty, speed, and low coordination burden.
- Challenge: Can I understand the next step and credible completion time immediately?
- Risk: urgency can make pressure tactics effective but unethical and damaging.

## Risk minimizer

- Trigger: consequential or unfamiliar decision.
- Optimizes: competence, process visibility, credentials, protection, recourse, and human access.
- Challenge: What happens if the provider is wrong, late, unavailable, or loses my data?
- Useful package: diagnostic, staged commitment, named owner, documented process, guarantee/redress.

## Comparison shopper

- Trigger: several plausible providers and enough time to compare.
- Optimizes: comparable scope, total price, proof, terms, and tradeoffs.
- Challenge: Is the low price created by exclusions, fees, lock-in, or lower service?
- Useful package: transparent good/better/best tiers with real boundaries—not decoys.

## Convenience optimizer

- Trigger: known job with low desire to manage it personally.
- Optimizes: easy intake, scheduling, payment, status visibility, and completion.
- Challenge: Does “done for me” hide customer work, delays, or handoffs?
- Useful package: concierge workflow, clear customer inputs, response-time promise.

## Advice and relationship seeker

- Trigger: ambiguity, personalization need, or long-lived consequence.
- Optimizes: objective advice, empathy, continuity, and fit.
- Challenge: Is the adviser independent; how are they paid; who owns my outcome?
- Useful package: paid diagnostic/advice, disclosed incentives, named adviser, written recommendation.

## Skeptical experienced buyer

- Trigger: prior disappointment or knowledge of common provider tactics.
- Optimizes: falsifiable proof, exclusions, independence, and control.
- Challenge: Show the bad-fit cases, limitations, complaints, and what you will not promise.
- Useful package: sample deliverable, references, transparent failure handling, easy exit.

## Context construction card

For each selected context define: trigger; painful job; desired progress; alternatives/current workaround; decision role; urgency; budget logic; time/effort budget; failure consequence; data sensitivity; proof threshold; disqualifiers; comparison set; purchase path; post-purchase anxiety; retention/referral condition.
