# Evidence Base

Last researched: 2026-07-06. Full source notes live in `research/topics/european-german-us-service-customer-psychology/`.

## Findings used by the skill

- The European Commission's 2025 Consumer Conditions Scoreboard reports widespread online concerns about targeted advertising, personal-data collection, scams, fake reviews, misleading discounts, and pressure selling. It supports trust-friction tests, not a single “European personality.”
- A European Commission behavioral study of non-life insurance found that comparison effort and biases impede decisions; clear costs, objective advice, independent/comprehensive comparison, and opportunities to pause and modify choices improved decision quality.
- OECD review evidence says authentic reviews can reduce search cost and help entrants, while fake, incentivized, suppressed, inaccurate, or unrepresentative reviews weaken trust.
- German sources make reviews, comparison transparency, privacy/security, contractual clarity, and provider identity plausible service-evaluation factors. The strongest practical warnings come from Verbraucherzentrale; vendor surveys remain directional.
- US FTC rules and guidance require truthful review/testimonial practices and address hidden fees and difficult cancellation. These are compliance constraints and trust-design inputs, not proof of conversion uplift.
- US local-service and trust surveys make review specificity/recency, convenience, and responsive service plausible hypotheses; use them cautiously because they are industry studies.

## Transfer limits

Evidence about retail, insurance, healthcare, subscriptions, and platforms does not automatically transfer to relocation, travel, advisory, home services, or other professional services. Geography interacts with service category, price, urgency, risk, regulation, age, channel, and prior experience. Validate the exact segment with behavior.
