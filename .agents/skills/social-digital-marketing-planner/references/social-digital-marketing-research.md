# Social / Digital Marketing Research Reference

Last researched: 2026-06-27

Use this reference as background for `social-digital-marketing-planner`. Treat these as planning principles, not fixed platform rules. When the user asks for the latest platform-specific execution details, search current official docs before making precise recommendations.

## Executive Summary

Strong social and digital marketing plans are built in this order: business goal, audience, offer, message, channel, content system, paid/organic mix, measurement, learning cadence. Weak plans usually start with channels, trends, posting frequency, or ad budget before validating audience behavior and conversion logic.

Across current sources, the durable best practices are:

- Start with 1-3 measurable business goals rather than generic "engagement".
- Choose platforms where the audience already spends time and where the brand can create native content consistently.
- Match content to funnel stage: awareness, trust, consideration, conversion, retention.
- Build repeatable content pillars and calendars, but keep room for timely response and community interaction.
- Treat creative as a performance lever. Test hooks, formats, proof, offers, CTAs, and audience angles.
- Avoid treating reach, likes, impressions, or follower count as proof of business impact.
- Measure incrementality where budget is meaningful enough; attribution alone can mislead.
- Add cultural, legal, claim-substantiation, and audience-sensitivity review before publishing risky campaigns.

## Best Practices

### Strategy

The American Marketing Association frames social strategy as a purposeful plan tied to business goals, audience research, platform selection, KPIs, content, scheduling, competitive analysis, and ongoing optimization. Use that sequence as the default skeleton for a plan.

Do not advise "be everywhere." Focus on the few platforms where audience behavior, content fit, and team capacity overlap.

### Creative And Content

Meta's official creative guidance emphasizes vertical or square video, concise text, movement, carousel use where relevant, and clear calls to action. Its delivery guidance also warns about creative fatigue and recommends refreshing creative, broadening audience, or using dynamic experiences when fatigue appears.

TikTok's official creative guidance says TikTok ads perform best when built for the platform: vertical 9:16, sound/music, visible safe zones, people/creators/employees/customers, less-polished native style, a hook early, proposition in the first few seconds, text overlays, transitions/graphics, strong CTA, and continuous testing. TikTok recommends diversified creative groups and refreshing when performance declines or daily new users are low.

For B2B, LinkedIn emphasizes relevance, trust, intent, quality over lead volume, thought leadership, professional targeting, and full-funnel campaigns. Its thought leadership guidance stresses credible expertise, useful/actionable content, executive or employee voices, decision-maker relevance, and measurement tied to objectives.

### Measurement

Google's incrementality guidance argues that incrementality testing is a stronger way to understand advertising's true business impact, especially when attribution and third-party signals are limited. It recommends annual testing plans, controlled experiments, and evaluating incremental revenue or profit against media spend.

Use the measurement ladder:

- Activity metrics: posts shipped, creative variants, response time.
- Attention metrics: reach, impressions, video views.
- Engagement metrics: comments, saves, shares, DMs, profile visits.
- Traffic metrics: sessions, landing-page views, click-through rate.
- Conversion metrics: leads, sales, bookings, trials, demos, cart adds.
- Business metrics: revenue, contribution margin, CAC, payback, retention, LTV, incremental ROAS.

Default to business metrics for paid campaigns and strategic reporting. Use attention/engagement metrics only as diagnostic signals unless the stated goal is awareness or community.

## Good Example Patterns

Use examples as patterns, not templates to copy.

- Spotify Wrapped: turns first-party user data into personalized, identity-relevant, highly shareable content. Lesson: make the audience the subject and make sharing feel self-expressive.
- Duolingo on TikTok: uses a recognizable brand character, platform-native humor, trend participation, and consistent personality. Lesson: distinctive assets and clear voice can compound, but only if the tone fits the brand and audience.
- Liquid Death: commits to a differentiated brand world in a low-interest category. Lesson: distinctiveness can create attention when the brand has a coherent point of view and accepts polarizing creative tradeoffs.
- B2B thought leadership: high-quality, useful expert content builds trust when buying risk is high. Lesson: serious buyers need education, credibility, and repeated exposure, not only lead forms.

## Bad Example Patterns

- Pepsi Kendall Jenner campaign: widely criticized for using protest imagery in a tone-deaf way. Marketing Week's analysis tied the failure to lack of outside perspective, insufficient market research, and failure to engage with affected communities.
- Burger King UK's "Women belong in the kitchen" tweet: intended as a campaign hook for a scholarship message but the standalone social post was read as sexist before context landed. Lesson: social posts are often consumed in isolation; risky irony and bait-first copy can fail even when the underlying cause is positive.
- Generic failure pattern: trend-jacking without brand permission, culture-jacking without stakeholder review, claims without proof, humor that punches down, and campaign mechanics that require too much context to understand.

## Planning Implications

Every detailed plan should include:

- Audience and buyer/persona.
- Customer pain, job, desire, or trigger.
- Platform role by funnel stage.
- Organic content pillars.
- Paid campaign structure and creative testing plan where relevant.
- Content calendar with cadence and owner.
- Measurement model with baselines, thresholds, and reporting cadence.
- Experiment backlog.
- Risk review for claims, cultural context, compliance, accessibility, privacy, and brand safety.
- 7-day action list plus 30/60/90-day roadmap.
