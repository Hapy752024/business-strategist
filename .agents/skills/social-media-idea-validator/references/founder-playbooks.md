## SaaS / B2B

### First customers come from three levers only — Lenny Rachitsky's founder research

Lenny's research across Figma, Stripe, Shopify, Plaid, Slack, Airtable, Gusto, Segment and others found essentially all early B2B growth comes from: (1) tapping your personal network, (2) seeking out customers where they already spend time (communities: Hacker News, Twitter/X, domain forums, open-source communities), and (3) press. Press rarely comes first. Personal network matters more when the product needs trust ("they bought the friendship, not the software" — Workday). Community-first entries worked when the founders were already of the community (Shopify and New Relic in Ruby on Rails, Segment and Dropbox via Hacker News).

Validation questions this raises: Is the founder already a member of the community they want to market into? If not, what earns admission? Who are the first 10 hand-picked users before any "channel" work?

Source: Lenny's Newsletter, "How today's fastest growing B2B businesses found their first ten customers" (2020), lennysnewsletter.com.

### Pain-Point SEO — Grow and Convert (agency founders, real SaaS conversion data)

High-volume keywords drive traffic that barely converts (0.03–0.19% in their client data); bottom-funnel, high-intent topics convert at 0.3–4.3%, and the highest-converting post in one client case targeted a keyword SEO tools showed as "zero volume". Prioritize, in order:

1. **Category keywords** — "best crm software", "<category> agency/tool": searcher is already in-market; inexcusable not to rank.
2. **Comparison / alternatives** — "salesforce vs hubspot", "<competitor> alternatives": deep-funnel switchers; under-utilized by most brands.
3. **Jobs-to-be-done keywords** — "how to manage sales leads": real buying intent; present the product as one solution among genuinely useful advice.

Skip top-of-funnel volume plays until the bottom is exhausted. Topic ideas come from customer/prospect language, gathered with their five survey questions: what problem were you solving before finding us; what would you use if we didn't exist; how would you describe us to a friend; top 3 benefits you get; what would you search for to research us. Sales/customer-facing teams are the shortcut when direct interviews are unavailable. Use contextual in-article CTAs tied to the post's intent.

Validation questions this raises: Does the content idea target a searcher with buying intent, or a reader who will never buy? Was the topic derived from customer language or invented in a boardroom?

Source: Benji Hyam, Grow and Convert, "Pain Point SEO: How to Produce SEO Content That Drives Conversions" (updated 2025), growandconvert.com.

## Fintech / Insurtech (Insurance Brokers And Financial Services)

### Policygenius — content as white space in a considered purchase

Co-founders Jennifer Fitzgerald and Francois de Lame built one of the leading insurtechs on organic content because incumbents "were all just buying AdWords on Google" and left content marketing open. Insurance is a considered, complicated purchase — "people do some reading before making a purchase" — so high-quality educational content compounds (they reported ~10% month-over-month traffic growth serving tens of thousands monthly). Content success required "a strong and unwavering belief in high-quality content", tracked as pages ranking on page one per vertical. They entered less brand-dominated verticals first (life, disability) and avoided P&C/auto ("we would have probably died on the hill" against Super Bowl brands). Their marketplace won on trust and open choice architecture, not on being cheapest to build.

Lessons: in complex, low-frequency financial purchases, education-first content is a durable moat; pick verticals/niches where brand ad spend doesn't drown you; measure rankings and conversion, not vanity reach.

Source: The Financial Revolutionist, "A Conversation with PolicyGenius' CEO Jennifer Fitzgerald" (2017), thefr.com.

### Ryan Hanley (The Murray Group) — answer real customer questions, relentlessly

An independent local insurance agency owner built national authority by producing content that answered the exact questions clients asked — the well-known "100 Insurance Questions in 100 Days" series (short videos answering one real question per day). Pattern: the agency's online presence accelerated "simply by producing more content" aimed at real customer questions. For brokers, the raw material is free: the questions clients ask every week.

Validation questions this raises: Can the founder list 50 real client questions right now? If not, the idea lacks its raw material — start by harvesting questions from inboxes and call logs.

Sources: SellTermLife podcast interview with Ryan Hanley; Flood Insurance Guru retrospective on the "100 Insurance Questions in 100 Days" series.

### Mortgage / loan officers — what practitioners say actually works (r/loanoriginators)

Practitioners on r/loanoriginators are blunt:

- **Face-to-face, calls, and hosted events** dominate: "the best thing is to call and get face to face and host marketing events. I spend my money on coffee and lunch." Mortgage is a relationship business; conversion happens in person.
- **Direct mail** gets specific praise: "There is no better borrower than one that calls you back from a mailer."
- **Ranked blog/video content** works when it generates inbound calls ("creating blog and video content that gets ranked online so they call me").
- **Generic social graphics are useless**: "no one goes to social media to see that." Reels/TikTok help only as *realtor/partner engagement* that sets up in-person meetings. Social "can become a whole job in itself"; there is only so much mortgage content before "cringe territory". Flyers: "may as well throw your money away."

Lessons for verdicts: for brokers and loan officers, social ideas should usually be validated as *assist* plays (credibility, partner engagement, retargeting pool) that set up calls and meetings — not as primary conversion channels. Expectation-setting belongs in the verdict (GO-as-assist).

Source: r/loanoriginators, "What marketing has worked best for YOU?" (2024).

## Cross-Cutting Rules For Idea Validation

1. **Audience presence before production.** If the segment is not on the platform in a receptive mindset, no format saves the idea.
2. **Demand evidence before production.** Real questions, real search intent, or a working comparable format — at least one, sourced. "I think people would watch this" is not evidence.
3. **Founder sustainability is a gate, not a preference.** A format the founder cannot produce weekly for 6 months dies by week 6. Prefer formats that reuse existing work (client questions, deal stories, underwriting explanations).
4. **Niche beats broad in trust businesses.** Own a slice (a vertical, a city, a customer type) where incumbents' ad spend and brand don't drown you — the Policygenius vertical-selection lesson.
5. **Education compounds in considered purchases; entertainment is rented attention.** For SaaS and financial services, useful content with buying intent outperforms reach plays on conversion per the Pain-Point SEO data.
6. **Compliance is a format constraint.** Insurance/financial promotion rules (state insurance advertising, FCA financial promotions, testimonial and claim substantiation) shape what can be published; flag compliance review as a production step for regulated verticals, not an afterthought.

## When To Re-Research

These patterns are durable, but platform mechanics, ad costs, and search behavior shift. Re-run source checks when: a verdict hinges on a specific platform's current algorithm or cost data; the target niche's competitive content has materially changed; or compliance rules in the target jurisdiction changed.
