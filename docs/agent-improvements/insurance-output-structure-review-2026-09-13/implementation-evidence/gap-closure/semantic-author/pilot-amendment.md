# Synthetic pilot amendment assessment

This is an assessment of the supplied synthetic observations. It neither starts a pilot nor authorizes implementation or outreach.

## Cancellation disclosure: admit the narrow amendment

Observed target interviewees could not understand a required cancellation disclosure. Supplied user testing shows comprehension after a short plain-language step. This meets the amendment rule: evidence connects the change to a required trust condition. The evidence supports comprehension in the tested setting; it does not establish purchase demand or generalize automatically to all future customers.

Amended current scope: add only the tested plain-language step alongside the required cancellation disclosure, preserving the required information. The minimum change addresses the observed comprehension failure; broader redesign is outside this amendment.

Implementation requires two hours. The supplied incremental support burden is one minute per customer against twenty spare support minutes per day. That allows at most twenty affected customers per day within that spare allowance, before extra rework or other demand. Actual daily volume and arrival peaks are unknown, so capacity fit remains conditional; a monthly sales capacity does not establish an even daily arrival pattern. The evidence does not establish legal sufficiency of revised wording; this amendment improves comprehension without asserting that it changes any disclosure requirement.

Validation: use the same substantive cancellation questions with target participants seeing the amended step, checking accurate explanation of cancellation conditions rather than a yes/no claim of understanding. Record comprehension failures, actual minutes, daily volume and support overruns. Preserve the amendment if comprehension remains correct and additional support stays within the stated spare allowance. Any material misunderstanding requires revision; a capacity overrun requires a delivery adjustment before wider use. The supplied successful test justifies the bounded amendment, while this follow-up checks whether the effect holds in use. Do not infer a conversion lift or payment validation.

History record to publish with the current scope: dated decision on 2026-09-13, prior scope without the added step, observed comprehension failure, tested remedy, two-hour implementation estimate, measured/assumed support burden and validation plan. The harness should publish current scope and the dated decision/history separately; this evaluation does not fabricate a live project history.

## Social leaderboard: defer

The founder cites competitor inclusion, without outcome or trust evidence. This does not meet the amendment rule. Three weeks of implementation would expand the pilot without resolving an evidenced customer requirement. Keep it out of current scope and record it as a deferred request, with the founder's rationale and the absence of supporting evidence. Reconsider only if evidence connects a specific leaderboard behavior to the chosen customer outcome or a required trust condition, and a smaller test can evaluate that claim with its cost and capacity effects explicit. Competitor presence alone is insufficient.
