# Inline behavioral sanity review

17 September 2026. Same-session author review against the 12 synthetic cases added to existing skill eval files. The outputs below are inline applications of the revised guidance, not independent model runs, actual customer research or a blind before/after comparison. The author knows the intended behavior. Deterministic routing is measured separately in `route-comparison.json`.

| Case ID suffix | Inline response to the synthetic packet | Review finding |
|---|---|---|
| latent-vs-low-urgency | Losing half a day establishes a consequential episode for those shops. It suggests a possible unmet outcome, but batching may be an acceptable trade-off. The satisfied shop is a useful contrast. Observe recent collection decisions and ask what, if anything, the shops tried to change; neither prevalence nor payment is established. | Preserves the inference, competing explanation, counterexample and next investigation. |
| adequate-vs-missing | The six observed shops appear adequately served for the stated job. The five unobserved shops remain unknown. There is no supported underserved pocket in the observed group, and no basis for a whole-market no-demand claim. | Distinguishes adequate service from absent coverage. |
| nonconsumption-falsified | Transport was not sufficient to explain adoption in this sample. The newly observed low valuation weakens that opportunity thesis. Investigate the remaining reasons or stop this branch; do not rename rejection as hidden demand. | Revises the causal hypothesis without denying that transport was difficult. |
| need-not-choice | Ordering time remains a problem, but the prepaid delivery proposition fails the shops' credit and quantity requirements. Test a promise that preserves those requirements if delivery economics permit; do not launch the rejected offer on the strength of pain alone. | Separates need from proposition choice. |
| subscription-not-moat | Recurring billing and message volume identify neither a measured economic benefit nor a barrier. Compare task performance with and without the proprietary records and against purchasable substitutes, then assess access, freshness and replication cost. Until then, the data moat is only a hypothesis; recurring economics also need retention and cost evidence. | Does not upgrade a label into defensibility. |
| counterposition-new-entrant | Cannibalization may deter the incumbent, but the new entrant has no such constraint. Test whether it could match cost and service at viable scale; also assess a separate incumbent unit. The incumbent's incentive conflict alone does not support a durable position. | Tests both rival types and an alternate incumbent response. |
| small-profitable-no-moat | Observed repeat purchases and contribution after owner labor support feasibility at the stated modest scale. No structural barrier is evidenced. Continue only with competitive exposure and cash/capacity limits visible; a moat is not a prerequisite for this income objective. | Avoids forced rejection and false protection. |
| clean-sheet-unaffordable | A 150,000 warehouse is infeasible with 15,000 available cash absent a credible financing path. Test the partner configuration's next-day performance, cost, reliability and exit terms. Keep the warehouse as a conditional later option, not the chosen model merely because picking is faster. | Distinguishes target design from feasible transition. |
| coherent-overserved | A fixed menu, batch purchasing and self-service ordering can reinforce a lower-cost promise for customers who accept those exclusions. Test actual choice, waste, exception demand and contribution. This is a coherent delivery hypothesis; neither novelty nor a proven moat is required. | Recognizes a valid sacrifice while preserving economic uncertainty. |
| cadence-only-no-expansion | Have the fulfilment owner review late-order count over orders due, oldest unresolved delay, cause and corrective action weekly. Use the agreed service target; if absent, set it explicitly before interpreting performance. Assign the next corrective action and follow-up date. No position or moat exercise is needed. | Remains within management cadence; no invented numerical threshold. |
| conflicting-promises | Eight service hours divided by four hours per bespoke job permits two jobs daily before overhead, versus the promise of ten. Revise scope, staffing, price or delivery time and test the change. Instant bespoke delivery at the lowest price has no supported configuration in this packet; an automation gain cannot be assumed. | Identifies the concrete conflict and returns to the promise. |
| complaints-not-demand | The count establishes discussion volume, not consequential unmet need or switching. Seek recent attributable episodes, consequences and current alternatives, then test urgency and choice. Scaling is unsupported by this packet. | Does not treat complaints as validated demand. |

Baseline instruction review found conflicting requirements in startup-building and risk guidance: mandatory moat identification, customer-top-three tests for barriers and broad B2C/B2B stereotypes. These were replaced by the shared benefit/barrier/value-capture method. This observed instruction correction is not an empirical estimate of output improvement.

Skill quality review: routing boundaries and existing IDs preserved; synthetic examples differ from the original video; new fields are optional; no host-specific core instructions or dependencies added; relevant entrypoints pass `quick_validate.py`. Codex/Claude Code/OpenCode live discovery and task behavior have not been separately tested. Structural portability is the limit of the current cross-host evidence. Token savings and independent strategic-quality improvement remain unmeasured.
