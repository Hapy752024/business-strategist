# Customer need and entrant success — adjustment plan

Updated 2026-09-07. Scope: reusable business-strategist workflow, separately from the insurance findings. Preserve existing skills, runners, registries and topic workspaces. No new agent or mandatory panel.

## Decision contract

For venture discovery, validation and recommendations, begin with a customer segment and need, including when the founder presents a product. Reuse supplied facts; ask only decision-changing questions. Express the hypothesis as: customer in a specific situation needs an outcome, currently uses an alternative, and experiences an evidenced shortfall. A feature remains a proposed response. Narrow execution requests for an already selected business do not restart discovery.

The central decision is whether this entrant can plausibly achieve the founder's definition of success within 12–24 months (or a different explicit deadline). Existing supply means served; it does not establish satisfactory outcomes, saturation, or inaccessible customers. “Fully served” is not a useful blanket verdict. Familiar products, effective distribution and several small service improvements may support a viable entrant. A proprietary moat is not required for a boutique business.

## Competitor assessment

Use one compact table per material comparison: `journey stage | customer need | observed competitor strength/weakness | source/date | proposed entrant advantage | test | confidence`.

- Visibility: does the target encounter the company when the need arises? Check relevant search results, social content, audience fit, referrals and other discovered channels. No social activity does not prove invisibility. Few followers do not prove easy entry.
- Sales: inspect content-to-offer-to-contact/application handoff, clarity, friction, trust, response and customer objections. Public journeys show design; actual conversion rates remain unknown without evidence.
- Service: examine onboarding, claims/problem handling, responsiveness and customer outcomes through balanced reviews and complaints. Distinguish intermediary and supplier responsibility.
- Retention/engagement: examine reasons customers return, service reminders, useful content, renewals, cross-needs and exits. Low public engagement does not prove poor retention; frequent engagement may be unnecessary in some categories.

Evaluate social media as a possible discovery and relationship channel, not a universal requirement. Show where initial exposure comes from, why people return, how they consent to further contact and what leads to purchase. Respect the founder's channel restrictions. A landing page alone is not a top-of-funnel plan.

## Exit criteria

A feature-first input produces a customer/need hypothesis. An occupied market is assessed for entry rather than automatically rejected. Every asserted competitor weakness has scoped evidence or is labeled a hypothesis. The 12–24-month assessment links customers and capacity to economics. Foreign cases distinguish outcome evidence from stories. Source failures and partial scope remain visible. README and manifest agree. No new infra is required beyond the small extension to the existing synthesis validator.

## Inline scenario review (not an independent model benchmark)

| Case | Required decision / reviewed result |
| --- | --- |
| Founder pitches an AI scheduling tool | Reconstruct whose scheduling problem, its trigger, alternative and consequence before assessing features. |
| Twenty cleaners serve a town; founder needs forty recurring clients | Assess reachable demand, capacity and contribution; competitor count alone cannot reject entry. |
| Small accountant has no Instagram but strong referrals | Social visibility is unknown/limited; total visibility and retention cannot be labeled weak from that observation. |
| Foreign SaaS founder reports revenue after a launch award | Verify time period, starting audience, revenue versus profit and retention; launch rank is not success proof. |
| Insurance keyword appears in stock-market roundup | Reject as evidence of the target customer's insurance decision; do not count it as customer pain. |
| Tests pass, but customer journeys/social review unfinished | Report research incomplete. Test results describe engineering checks only. |
