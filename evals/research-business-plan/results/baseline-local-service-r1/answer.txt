The provisional value proposition is “less effort and clearer choices during a home-care decision.” Its assumed customer jobs are finding suitable options and understanding tradeoffs; its assumed pains are uncertainty and comparison effort. The business-model hypotheses are household customers, manual comparison delivery, permission-based referral channels and possible household fees. None is validated.

**Exact next tests — proposed, not authorized**

1. **Unpaid discovery:** propose 12 voluntary interviews in one locality: six elderly people involved in a recent care decision and six family decision-makers, preferably from different households. Ask about the last actual decision, alternatives used, time spent, unresolved difficulties, trust and who paid. Avoid pitching until the experience is documented. A proposed continuation threshold is four independent households describing a concrete unresolved comparison difficulty; this would justify further investigation, not establish demand.
2. **Alternatives audit:** inspect five locally available providers or advice routes through official materials. Record scope, eligibility, fees where disclosed, coverage and information dates. If an accessible existing service already resolves the reported difficulty, revise the proposition before prototyping.
3. **Unpaid prototype test:** after discovery supports a specific problem, test a fictional, clearly labelled comparison with five new eligible participants. Ask them to explain its tradeoffs and missing information. Proposed pass: four correctly understand the comparison and no material misunderstanding remains unresolved. This tests comprehension, not willingness to pay.
4. **Delivery and economics test:** time three fictional comparison exercises and document verification gaps. Obtain founder availability, budget and income requirements; leave unsupported financial inputs blank. Do not calculate viability until material inputs have defensible sources.
5. **Commercial gate:** only after these findings and a separate execution decision, propose a transparent paid-offer test with a declared price, delivery scope, cost ceiling and stop rule. Stated interest alone would not pass a payment test.

Research should use customer accounts of actual decisions, official Swiss provider and advice-service materials, relevant Swiss statistical sources for geographically appropriate counts, and official legal/regulatory sources. Primary company records would be needed to investigate the US analog and Japanese closure. An uncapped, documented VC comparison would be needed to assess funding direction; VC research is secondary for this local service.
