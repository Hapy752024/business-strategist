## The Sequencing Rule

Supported by HEC Paris (300+ B2B high-tech startups), YC, First Round, GetYourGuide, and Xiaohongshu sources:

1. **Manual learning** — direct selling, manual delivery, founder-led motion.
2. **Proof** — retained customers, repeat purchase, contribution economics.
3. **One repeatable acquisition motion** — the binding-constraint lever, not a channel checklist.
4. **Scale** — broader marketing and partnerships only after 1–3 hold.

Applying this in any skill: if a plan skips from step 1 to step 4, flag it. Paid amplification before retained-value proof is the canonical failure mode (YC's Socialcam case: large downloads and traffic coexisted with poor retention and no durable PMF).

Slack, Stripe, Figma, Calendly, and Monzo add operational detail: recruit in small batches, personally remove onboarding friction, compare requests with observed use, and let increasingly demanding cohorts expose the next constraint. Superhuman adds a useful segmentation method: study the customers who would most miss the product and the near-fit users who value the same core benefit. Its 40% survey threshold is a heuristic, not a replacement for retention, payment, referrals, or contribution economics.

## The Weak-Evidence List

The following are **weak evidence** until connected to retained customers or contribution economics:

- Downloads, installs, signups, waitlist size
- Views, likes, followers, newsletter subscribers
- Press coverage and launch spikes
- Partnership announcements (vs. measured partner-driven retained customers)

Any strategy that quotes these as traction must name them as weak evidence and re-anchor on proof: retention, repeat purchase, paid pilots, referral, contribution margin.

## Channel Jobs, Not Channel Checklists

Select a channel only after naming its job: discovery, education/trust, demand capture, conversion, activation, retention, referral, community, or recruiting. A study of 1,346 users across multiple networks found platform engagement is context-specific; “social media” is not a homogeneous medium.

- Instagram: visual proof, identity/lifestyle, creator/customer stories, and local discovery; not a complete path for complex high-trust financial conversion.
- TikTok: discovery and native short-video creative learning; requires creative cadence and an education/conversion bridge for considered purchases.
- YouTube: searchable education, demos, comparisons, and long-lived trust assets; slower to produce and compound than direct first-customer work.
- LinkedIn: B2B founder/expert credibility, account education, trigger-based outreach, recruiting, and qualified pipeline; weak when used for generic corporate posting or broad B2C reach.
- Meta/Facebook: paid reach, retargeting, local services, older audiences, and some established groups; requires retained-value and incrementality gates before scale.
- X: founder voice, real-time tech/media niches, and support; use only when the audience and founder operating style fit.
- Reddit, Hacker News, and specialist forums: problem learning, feedback, and credible niche entry through useful participation; promotion-first behavior backfires.
- Search/SEO: capture explicit intent and educate complex purchases; weak for an unknown category without a category-education plan.
- Email: owned activation, education, conversion, retention, and referral; it is not discovery without an acquired list.
- Creators/podcasts: borrow credible niche trust; choose for audience/product fit and qualified downstream behavior, not follower count.
- Product surfaces: recipient exposure, collaboration, and referral are growth loops, not channels. Measure every loop step through recipient retention and abuse.

Current platform specifications, algorithms, targeting, costs, and regulated-advertising rules must be retrieved before execution.

## Channel and Loop Recommendation Protocol

Before recommending any channel or growth loop, answer all eight questions (from the founder case matrix in `founder-growth-channel-patterns-2026.md`):

1. **Stage:** Are we learning, validating retained value, finding repeatability, or scaling?
2. **Customer context:** Where does the trigger occur and where do customers already seek help?
3. **Channel job:** Discovery, education/trust, capture, conversion, activation, retention, referral, community, or recruiting?
4. **Mechanism:** What customer behavior connects exposure to value?
5. **Prerequisites:** Which assets, credibility, cadence, product behavior, compliance, and measurement must exist?
6. **Cohort gate:** How will activation, retention, contribution, support, fraud, and complaints be compared by source?
7. **Transfer limit:** Which founder-specific advantage (network, reputation, audience, product workflow, timing, regulatory context) makes the source case non-portable?
8. **Stop rule:** What result means defer, change the message/offer, change the channel, or fix the product?

A recommendation that cannot answer 6–8 is a hypothesis, not a plan — cap the budget and name the evidence that would unlock more. Every substantial recommendation names one primary acquisition motion, one supporting/owned motion, any product/community loop, deferred channels, and the evidence gate that unlocks scale.

## Product and Referral Loop Gates

Founder cases show four recurring loop structures:

1. **Recipient exposure** — Calendly scheduling links and Typeform responses show value to a necessary participant.
2. **Collaboration** — Slack and Figma become more useful when a real colleague joins a shared workflow.
3. **Reciprocal/product-currency reward** — Dropbox rewarded both parties with storage.
4. **Scarcity and network utility** — Monzo's Golden Tickets followed real use, while friend payment features increased usefulness; Robinhood's queue created launch demand but still required funded and retained accounts.

Recommend a loop only when value precedes the invitation, the recipient is relevant, the next user experiences value quickly, attribution and fraud controls exist, and referred cohorts retain with viable contribution economics. Do not call a share button, contact upload, paid signup, or queue position a viral loop without these properties.

Referral evidence is heterogeneous. A German-bank field study found referred customers at least 16% more valuable in its setting; a Chilean-bank replication found greater loyalty without necessarily greater value. Use the mechanism as a hypothesis and test incrementality by segment rather than importing the percentage.

## Community Operating Model

Community means repeated member-to-member value, not followers, registered members, or a branded group.

1. Define a narrow member identity, recurring problem, and useful exchange.
2. Recruit the founding cohort manually and seed a ritual such as critique, office hours, peer answer, benchmark, or local event.
3. Keep the founder/operator present and show when member feedback changes the product.
4. Add newcomer paths, roles, recognition, moderation, and safety before broad growth.
5. Connect participation to customer outcomes without turning every exchange into a pitch.
6. Expand only when useful interactions occur without staff initiating all of them.

Track member-to-member reply rate, time to first useful response, return contribution, activity not initiated by staff, customer/product outcomes, moderation load, and safety. A six-month field experiment supports both identity-based and bond-based attachment features; a 2024 survey of 951 Reddit community founders found information, topical interest, and connection more common than self-promotion and emphasized interaction quality over size.

Community anti-patterns: empty Discord/Slack groups, broadcast audiences called communities, self-promotion as the primary exchange, optimizing member count or posting volume, weak moderation, and asking for engagement without acting on input. Figma's sequencing is an important counterexample to “community first”: the founders initially imagined community first but were pushed to establish a useful business tool before the community layer.

## Low-Cost Growth Versus “Hacks”

Low-cash growth is defensible when it increases learning or compounds customer value: founder recruitment/onboarding, useful participation in concentrated communities, customer-language content, high-intent SEO, native repurposing of one strong artifact, invitations after activation, product-currency rewards, product-recipient exposure, manual partner handoffs, and substantiated transparency/mission.

Do not recommend spam, fake scarcity, manufactured controversy, mass unsolicited automation, dark patterns, or regulatory arbitrage. Founder-specific advantages must be named: Dropbox had technical-community fit; Stripe had dense YC access and credibility; Buffer had a relevant founder audience; Monzo had a strong story, timing, and media access. Copy the mechanism only if the prerequisites exist.

## Marketing Measurement Guardrails

- Compare activation, retention, contribution, support, refunds/claims, fraud, and complaints by acquisition source.
- Use paid spend as a capped learning test before it becomes a scale motion.
- For material budgets, prefer randomized, geo, holdout, or lift designs. Large eBay paid-search experiments and 15 Facebook advertising experiments show observational attribution can substantially misstate incrementality.
- Brand-keyword paid search may intercept organic demand; separate new/infrequent from existing users.
- Report attributed platform results as diagnostic data, not causal business impact.

## Service-Customer Decision and Trust

From the Europe/Germany/US service-customer research (European Commission 2025 Consumer Conditions Scoreboard and behavioral insurance experiment, OECD review evidence, German consumer-protection warnings, US FTC guidance). These are cross-skill checks for any skill that designs offers, messaging, funnels, or campaigns for consumer services:

- **Uncertainty reducers scale with risk.** Consequential, unfamiliar, data-sensitive, or hard-to-reverse services need precise scope, process visibility, credentials, relevant proof, total price upfront, human access, and redress. A marketing plan for such a service that leads with lifestyle claims instead of risk reducers is addressing the wrong anxiety.
- **Trigger/job/risk context beats personas.** A trigger, job-to-be-done, and risk context predicts service choice better than a nationality-based persona. Geography is a contextual layer applied *after* trigger, job, risk, price, and category. Never turn a national average into an individual's personality, and never present simulated customer dialogue as research.
- **Reviews work under conditions.** Recent, service- and segment-specific, independently hosted or verifiable reviews — accompanied by the provider's response to failure — build trust; a star average alone does not. Fake, incentivized, or suppressed reviews are documented trust-destroyers (OECD, Verbraucherzentrale) and legal violations (US FTC material-connection and authenticity rules).
- **Stage the commitment.** When outcome variance and expertise asymmetry are high, a staged package or paid diagnostic outperforms a large irreversible commitment. The EC insurance experiment found clear cost display, objective advice, independent comparison, and the ability to pause and modify choices improved decision quality.
- **Exit clarity is a lead-quality lever.** Clear cancellation, refund, data-use, and complaint pathways may reduce superficial conversion while improving lead quality and decision quality. Treat hidden fees, hard cancellation, misleading discounts, and pressure selling as compliance constraints and trust failures, not conversion tactics.
- **Judge marketing on qualified commitment.** Success is qualified commitment and delivery outcomes, not clicks or a persona's stated approval.

Interpretation limit: these are hypotheses until tested on the idea's exact category, price, trigger, channel, and buyer; the underlying category evidence is concentrated in insurance, healthcare, subscriptions, ecommerce, and platforms. For a full challenge pass on a specific service, use `service-customer-perspective-challenger`.

## Pull Signals and Pivots

Inbound demand from a segment the founder did not target is a pivot signal worth a small paid test before committing (GetYourGuide: professional suppliers asking—and one paying—to accelerate listing preceded the pivot from student tours to professional experiences). Do not ignore pull, and do not treat a handful of inbound requests as proof either.

## Community Before Commerce (China)

The strongest sourced China case (Xiaohongshu) sequenced expert-informed content → UGC/review community → direct commerce with licensed brands. China recommendations carry lower confidence: direct Chinese-language founder coverage was thin, and current local regulatory/platform expertise is required before execution.

## Regional Expansion

Europe requires country-level validation: customer language, trust, licensing, and distribution partners vary by market. Regulated categories (fintech, insurtech, professional services) cannot separate GTM from licensing, advice/suitability, complaints, and partner-control responsibilities; EIOPA documents national innovation hubs and sandboxes rather than a uniform EU shortcut.

## Interpretation Limits

- Named examples are mostly survivors; survivorship and selection bias remain substantial.
- Founder retrospectives simplify causality; historical channel tactics may no longer price or rank the same way.
- Academic findings often come from a single company, older platforms, or stated intentions; use them for mechanisms and boundary conditions, not universal benchmarks.
- No universal numeric activation, retention, CAC, or payback benchmarks transfer safely across archetypes.
- Jurisdiction-specific requirements must be retrieved fresh for each regulated launch.
