## The Sequencing Rule

Supported by HEC Paris (300+ B2B high-tech startups), YC, First Round, GetYourGuide and Xiaohongshu sources:

1. **Manual learning**: direct selling, manual delivery, founder-led motion.
2. **Proof**: retained customers, repeat purchase, contribution economics.
3. **One repeatable acquisition motion**: the binding-constraint lever, not a channel checklist.
4. **Scale**: broader marketing and partnerships only after 1–3 hold.

If a plan skips from step 1 to step 4, flag it. Paid amplification before retained-value proof is the canonical failure mode (YC's Socialcam: large downloads with poor retention). Superhuman's 40% survey threshold is a heuristic, not a replacement for retention, payment, referrals or contribution economics.

## The Weak-Evidence List

Weak evidence until connected to retained customers or contribution economics: downloads, installs, signups, waitlist size; views, likes, followers, subscribers; press and launch spikes; partnership announcements. A strategy that quotes these as traction must name them as weak and re-anchor on retention, repeat purchase, paid pilots, referral or contribution margin.

## Channel Jobs, Not Channel Checklists

Select a channel only after naming its job: discovery, education/trust, demand capture, conversion, activation, retention, referral, community or recruiting. Platform engagement is context-specific (study of 1,346 users). Typical fits: Instagram for visual proof and local discovery; TikTok for discovery and short-video creative learning; YouTube for searchable education and long-lived trust; LinkedIn for B2B credibility and trigger-based outreach; Meta paid for reach, retargeting and local services, gated on retained value and incrementality; X for founder voice in real-time niches; Reddit, Hacker News and forums for problem learning through useful participation (promotion-first backfires); search for explicit intent; email for owned activation and retention (not discovery); creators/podcasts for borrowed niche trust; product surfaces as loops, not channels. Retrieve current platform specifications, costs and regulated-advertising rules before execution.

## Channel and Loop Recommendation Protocol

Before recommending any channel or growth loop, answer:

1. **Stage:** learning, validating retained value, finding repeatability or scaling?
2. **Customer context:** where does the trigger occur and where do customers already seek help?
3. **Channel job.**
4. **Mechanism:** what customer behavior connects exposure to value?
5. **Prerequisites:** assets, credibility, cadence, product behavior, compliance, measurement.
6. **Cohort gate:** how will activation, retention, contribution, support, fraud and complaints be compared by source?
7. **Transfer limit:** which founder-specific advantage makes the source case non-portable?
8. **Stop rule:** what result means defer, change message/offer, change channel or fix the product?

A recommendation that cannot answer 6–8 is a hypothesis, not a plan: cap the budget and name the evidence that would unlock more. Name one primary acquisition motion, one supporting/owned motion, any loop, deferred channels and the gate that unlocks scale.

## Product and Referral Loop Gates

Recurring loop structures: recipient exposure (Calendly, Typeform), collaboration (Slack, Figma), reciprocal product-currency reward (Dropbox), scarcity plus network utility (Monzo; Robinhood's queue still needed funded, retained accounts). Recommend a loop only when value precedes the invitation, the recipient is relevant, the next user gets value quickly, attribution and fraud controls exist, and referred cohorts retain with viable contribution. A share button, contact upload or queue position is not a viral loop without these. Referral value differs by setting (German bank: referred customers ≥16% more valuable; Chilean replication: more loyal, not necessarily more valuable); test incrementality by segment.

## Community Operating Model

Community means repeated member-to-member value, not followers or a branded group. Define a narrow member identity and recurring useful exchange; recruit the founding cohort manually and seed a ritual; keep the founder present and show when feedback changes the product; add newcomer paths and moderation before broad growth; expand only when useful interactions occur without staff starting them. Track member-to-member reply rate, time to first useful response, return contribution, staff-independent activity, outcomes and moderation load. Anti-patterns: empty groups, broadcast audiences called communities, self-promotion as the main exchange, optimizing member count. Figma established a useful tool before the community layer.

## Low-Cost Growth Versus "Hacks"

Low-cash growth is defensible when it increases learning or compounds customer value (founder onboarding, useful community participation, customer-language content, high-intent SEO, post-activation invitations, manual partner handoffs). Do not recommend spam, fake scarcity, manufactured controversy, mass unsolicited automation, dark patterns or regulatory arbitrage. Name founder-specific advantages (Dropbox's technical community, Stripe's YC access, Buffer's audience, Monzo's story and timing); copy the mechanism only if the prerequisites exist.

## Marketing Measurement Guardrails

- Compare activation, retention, contribution, support, refunds, fraud and complaints by acquisition source.
- Use paid spend as a capped learning test before it becomes a scale motion.
- For material budgets prefer randomized, geo, holdout or lift designs; eBay and Facebook experiments show observational attribution can substantially misstate incrementality.
- Brand-keyword paid search may intercept organic demand.
- Report attributed platform results as diagnostic data, not causal business impact.

## Service-Customer Decision and Trust

From European Commission 2025 Consumer Conditions Scoreboard and insurance experiment, OECD review evidence, German consumer-protection warnings and US FTC guidance:

- **Uncertainty reducers scale with risk.** Consequential, unfamiliar, data-sensitive or hard-to-reverse services need precise scope, process visibility, credentials, relevant proof, total price upfront, human access and redress.
- **Trigger/job/risk context beats personas.** Geography is a layer applied after trigger, job, risk, price and category. Never turn a national average into an individual's personality, and never present simulated customer dialogue as research.
- **Reviews work under conditions:** recent, segment-specific, independently hosted or verifiable, with the provider's response to failure. Fake, incentivized or suppressed reviews destroy trust and break FTC rules.
- **Stage the commitment** when outcome variance and expertise asymmetry are high (staged package or paid diagnostic).
- **Exit clarity is a lead-quality lever.** Hidden fees, hard cancellation, misleading discounts and pressure selling are compliance constraints and trust failures, not conversion tactics.
- **Judge marketing on qualified commitment** and delivery outcomes, not clicks or a persona's stated approval.

These are hypotheses until tested on the exact category, price, trigger, channel and buyer.

## Pull Signals, China and Regional Expansion

Inbound demand from an untargeted segment is a pivot signal worth a small paid test (GetYourGuide), not proof. China (Xiaohongshu) sequenced expert content → UGC/review community → commerce; China recommendations carry lower confidence and need current local regulatory expertise. Europe requires country-level validation; regulated categories (fintech, insurtech, professional services) cannot separate GTM from licensing, suitability, complaints and partner control.

## Interpretation Limits

Named examples are mostly survivors; founder retrospectives simplify causality; academic findings often come from one company or older platforms. No universal activation, retention, CAC or payback benchmarks transfer across archetypes. Retrieve jurisdiction-specific requirements fresh for each regulated launch.
