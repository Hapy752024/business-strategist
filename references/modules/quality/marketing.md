# Marketing output review rubric

Review the message against a real buying context. Engagement and polished copy do not prove demand.

## Good example

“For Swiss independent electricians who lose time chasing unsigned estimates, the message promises a clearer follow-up queue. Evidence: three reviewed interviews describe manual reminders; none tested a paid product. Test the promise in five sales calls and record whether owners share a recent example, who approves spend and what they currently use. Continue only if the test reveals a reachable buyer and a concrete next commitment.”

Why it is good: it binds audience and promise to evidence, labels the gap, and defines an observable test and decision.

## Poor example

“Use urgency, social proof and AI personalization on LinkedIn to drive conversions.”

Why it is poor: it supplies tactics without a buyer, substantiated proof, channel access, hypothesis or learning rule.

## Review questions

- Is the audience specific and supported by the selected brief or current business decision?
- Can each claim and proof point be traced to evidence the audience can trust?
- Does the offer address the buyer’s present alternative and switching concerns?
- Is the channel reachable at plausible effort and cost, with spend authorization kept separate?
- Does the campaign state its hypothesis, measure, stop/continue rule and next decision?
