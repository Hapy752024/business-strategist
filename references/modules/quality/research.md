# Research output review rubric

Use this rubric for market discovery and idea-validation outputs. Do not turn it into a numerical demand score.

## Good example

“Two German practice managers described manually checking missed appointments each Monday; one paid an employee about an hour weekly to do it. The sample is small and one account came through a software vendor, so it supports a recurring workflow hypothesis, not prevalence. One practice reported no meaningful problem because its existing system sends reminders. Next, interview independent practices about the last missed-appointment review and who owns the task.”

Why it is good: it names a segment and episode, separates observed accounts from interpretation, preserves a counterexample and sampling limit, and proposes a discriminating next check.

## Poor example

“Clinics need AI appointment automation. The market is underserved because no local startup offers it.”

Why it is poor: it invents a solution preference, ignores current alternatives and satisfied users, and treats competitor absence as demand.

## Review questions

- Can every consequential statement be traced to reviewed source spans and the right study?
- Are user, buyer, payer, supplier, and intermediary roles distinguished when known?
- Are actual alternatives, resolved complaints, successful workarounds, contrary cases and source gaps included?
- Does the conclusion match the sampled segment, geography and evidence strength?
- Are interpretation and unknowns visible, and is the next step capable of changing the conclusion?
