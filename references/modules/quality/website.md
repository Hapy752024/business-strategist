# Website output review rubric

Review the rendered website and a key visitor task. Source code and a successful build are not user-task evidence.

## Good example

“A first-time visitor from the German trades landing page can identify the service, see the evidence-backed eligibility limits and reach the quote request in four steps on mobile. The price example matches the approved brief. Keyboard focus is visible; one consent link is unreachable from the footer and blocks launch until fixed.”

Why it is good: it tests a real rendered task, checks claim fidelity and accessibility, and names a release-blocking defect.

## Poor example

“The homepage is modern, responsive and SEO-friendly.”

Why it is poor: those labels do not establish comprehension, task completion, claim accuracy, accessibility or search performance.

## Review questions

- Can a target visitor understand the offer, audience and next step in the rendered page?
- Do published numbers, comparisons and promises match the approved evidence and brief?
- Can the key task be completed with keyboard and narrow-screen layouts?
- Are accessibility, forms, navigation, links, consent and error states checked where relevant?
- Are build/browser observations distinguished from production traffic or conversion results?
