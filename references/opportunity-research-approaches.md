# Complementary research approaches for idea validation and customer problems

Use this guidance for substantive work when the user wants to pressure-test a specific idea or understand a customer segment's problems about a topic. The user-selected question controls scope throughout. Do not turn research into a search for companies to imitate.

## Keep the two intents explicit

- **Idea validation:** retain the user's proposed idea as the hypothesis under review. Examine its customer, problem, alternatives and material risks. Evidence may support, qualify or contradict it.
- **Customer-problem understanding:** explain the segment's situations, tasks, outcomes, consequences, workarounds and variation for the requested topic. The result can end with findings and open questions without proposing a product or venture.

Reuse the router's existing market-discovery versus idea-validation choice. Ask one scope question only when intent genuinely changes the work and cannot be inferred. Narrow factual lookups remain narrow.

Similar companies in the same or other countries are optional comparison sources. Examine them when they help explain the user's question: what customer situation they address, how customers handle it, alternatives, switching behavior, change events, and relevant counterexamples. Preserve company location, customer-market evidence, target-segment fit and time period as separate facts. A foreign offer, revenue assertion, marketplace listing or founder story is not proof of the same problem or demand in the user's segment. Cross-country adaptation is in scope only when requested.

## Choose distinct evidence approaches

Select the smallest set that can address the question. Methods can run in parallel when they search different source frames or test different explanations. Topic-led customer voice remains required for substantive customer-input work; an entity-led competitor pass supplements it where known companies exist, according to `references/customer-voice.md`.

| Approach | Research question it serves | Existing owners |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer workflow | What happens in the customer's actual situation? What outcome, pain, workaround, success or vocabulary do they describe without prompting them with a vendor name? | `market-problem-discovery`, `evidence-scout`, `interview-bridge` |
| Alternatives and comparable cases | What does this segment actually use, including manual labor, existing software, services and no change? When companies in other markets address a relevant similar problem, what is comparable and what differs? | `competitive-landscape-builder`, `competitor-scout`, `business-archetype-playbook-researcher` when founder history can inform the question |
| Switching and change events | What triggered a search or change? What did users and buyers do after a price/package/product change, integration failure or shutdown? Why did others stay? | `competitor-monitoring`, `evidence-scout`, `interview-bridge` |
| Ecosystem and implementation | Which connected tools, integrations, marketplace tasks or repeated implementation jobs illuminate the customer workflow? Is the evidence from an end customer, supplier or intermediary? | `competitive-landscape-builder`, `evidence-scout`, pilot/design owners when a feasibility test is needed |

Founder lived experience can seed a query when the user supplies it; attribute it and check it against target-customer evidence. Named founder histories and acquisition mechanisms are conditional evidence for commercial or distribution questions, not mandatory output for a pain-point study.

## Parallel work protocol

1. Coordinator records intent, exact user question, target segment, geography/language when relevant, supplied idea/hypothesis, known facts and open alternatives.
2. Assign each worker a distinct approach, decision question, source families and isolated output location. Workers should not inherit another worker's preferred conclusion. Keep shared research artifacts and source/authorization constraints.
3. Each worker returns source locators and relevant captured passages; observation versus interpretation; source role; date and customer fit; candidate/finding/question links; counterexamples and coverage gaps. Do not return only an opinion or candidate list.
4. One coordinator merges. Deduplicate source identities, customer incidents and copied announcements across workers. Unknown identity stays unknown. Repeated discovery is not independent corroboration.
5. Compare evidence coverage for the chosen question, including alternative explanations and absent or satisfactory cases. Payment, buyer authority, market size, acquisition and delivery economics are examined when material to validating this user's idea, not mechanically required for every pain-point explanation.
6. Assign a challenge pass against the actual conclusion. Look for evidence the proposed pain is absent, adequately handled, resolved or different by context; for idea validation, test the user's premise and conditions that would falsify it. Read original sources. Model agreement is not customer evidence.
7. Preserve uncertainty and disagreements. Choose another retrieval, interview or feasibility step only where it could change the answer. A source/access failure is a coverage gap.
8. Publish one synthesis for the user-selected purpose. Do not automatically offer a different idea, transfer a product from a foreign market or advance to business commitments.

Parallel workers should have separate output paths and a sole synthesis owner. Do not launch extra workers when they will inspect the same sources without a distinct question. Workload, source budgets and paid API authorizations keep their existing scope.

## Output rules

For pain-point work, describe specific situations, triggers, tasks, desired outcomes, observed consequences, workarounds and alternatives that work. Show meaningful segment differences, positive/satisfactory experiences, contrary evidence, scope and unknowns. Keep observed accounts distinct from inferred patterns.

For idea validation, map the user's assumptions to supporting evidence, contrary evidence and unresolved questions. Include actual alternatives and switching context. Distinguish user, buyer, budget holder and approver where it affects this idea. Make a conclusion proportional to evidence; a weak idea may be rejected.

Use comparable companies as examples and evidence only with source role, customer-market evidence, context and applicability limits. Do not infer profit from portfolio membership, customer demand from pricing, traction from hiring, revenue from traffic, or willingness to switch from a complaint.

Only add commercial feasibility, founder playbooks, pricing/pilots, positioning or recommended business models when the user asks or they materially resolve the idea being validated. Respect the existing Business gate and outreach/recruitment controls.
