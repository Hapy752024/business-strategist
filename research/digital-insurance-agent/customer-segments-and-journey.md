# Customer Segments And Journey

## 1. Customer Definition

The initial customer is not "any foreigner in Germany." The target customer is:

> A high-earning, non-native resident in Germany who faces a high-stakes personal insurance decision and does not fully trust or understand the existing broker, comparison-portal, or insurer explanation.

Primary products:

- Private health insurance / public health insurance decision: PKV vs GKV.
- Occupational disability insurance: BU.
- Term life insurance: Risikolebensversicherung.
- Critical illness / dread disease cover.
- Later: employer benefits, bAV, private pension, claims and contract management.

Primary languages:

- Mandarin.
- English.
- German.

## 2. Segment Priority

### Segment A: Mandarin-Speaking High-Earning Professionals

Who they are:

- Chinese citizens or Mandarin-speaking residents in Germany.
- Engineers, data/AI workers, finance professionals, consultants, researchers, founders, senior PhDs/postdocs transitioning into industry.
- Often in Germany long enough to face real insurance decisions, not just visa/student health insurance.

Main triggers:

- First full-time job after study/PhD.
- Salary approaches or exceeds JAEG.
- Move from expat/student insurance to statutory or private health insurance.
- Marriage or child planning.
- Considering BU after hearing German colleagues mention it.
- Existing broker/agent recommendation in German that they cannot confidently evaluate.

Pain points:

- German insurance terms are hard to map into Mandarin concepts.
- Public Mandarin content skews toward students and basic health insurance.
- Trust depends heavily on community/referral.
- They may understand English, but high-stakes fine print is easier in Mandarin.
- They may not know whether advice is independent or sales-led.

Why this is the first non-English wedge:

- Cleaner language wedge than India/Hindi.
- Existing English expat brokers do not fully own Mandarin trust.
- WeChat/community distribution can be powerful if accessed credibly.

Risks:

- Public demand signal is weak; much demand may be hidden in private communities.
- Segment may be smaller than English-speaking high-earner expats.
- Requires native-quality Mandarin and cultural fluency.

### Segment B: English-Speaking High-Earning Foreign Professionals

Who they are:

- Indian, US, UK, EU, and other international professionals in Germany.
- Tech, finance, consulting, research, startup, product, engineering roles.
- Many are comfortable with English but not German insurance law and product terms.

Main triggers:

- Blue Card / skilled-worker move.
- Salary increase above JAEG.
- First German broker conversation.
- Comparing Feather, ottonova, CLARK, CHECK24, Verivox.
- Existing PKV offer that feels cheaper but risky.

Pain points:

- They can read English pages but still do not understand long-term trade-offs.
- They struggle with whether Germany is a short-term or long-term home.
- Family, spouse, children, and return-to-home-country scenarios matter.
- They do not know if German BU is relevant compared with savings or employer benefits.

Why this matters:

- Larger high-earner pool than Mandarin.
- Easier content production.
- More competitive because Feather and expat sites already serve it.

### Segment C: German-Speaking High Earners Who Distrust Broker Incentives

Who they are:

- German residents above JAEG.
- Self-employed professionals.
- Beamte/Referendare.
- High-income couples.

Main triggers:

- PKV/GKV decision.
- Premium increase.
- Broker recommendation they cannot verify.
- BU health question anxiety.

Pain points:

- Distrust of commissions.
- Fear of irreversible PKV decision.
- Tariff complexity.
- Conflicting advice from brokers, Reddit, YouTube, Finanzfluss, Finanztip, friends.

Why this matters:

- Strongest public evidence is in German forums and Reddit.
- Useful for content proof and product-market learning.
- It reduces dependency on the Mandarin wedge.

### Segment D: Families, Homeowners, And Couples

Who they are:

- High earners with spouse, child planning, mortgage, or one-income risk.
- Foreign or German.

Main triggers:

- Marriage.
- Pregnancy/children.
- House/apartment purchase.
- Mortgage.
- Partner reducing work.

Pain points:

- PKV family economics.
- Term life coverage amount.
- BU coverage adequacy.
- What happens if one income disappears.
- Need to coordinate policies rather than buy separately.

Use later:

- This segment is valuable but should follow PKV/BU second-opinion validation.

### Segment E: Existing PKV Customers With Admin Or Claims Pain

Who they are:

- People already in PKV.
- Beamte and high earners.
- Customers receiving invoices, reimbursement delays, premium increases, or confusing doctor bills.

Main triggers:

- Premium shock.
- Doctor invoice not reimbursed.
- High GOÄ multiplier requiring justification.
- Claim delay.
- Tariff change discussion.

Pain points:

- "I miss just showing the health card."
- Reimbursement follow-up.
- Document chaos.
- Advisor disappeared after sale.

Use later:

- Strong fit for app retention and claims support, but less ideal as first acquisition wedge unless a claim-support offer is tested separately.

## 4. Customer Journey

### Stage 1: Latent Awareness

Customer state:

- Insurance is boring and avoided.
- Customer assumes employer, public system, or existing policy is enough.
- Non-native customer may not know BU or PKV implications.

Trigger content:

- "In Germany, high income creates a health-insurance decision."
- "BU is one of the insurance products foreigners often discover late."
- "If you plan children, PKV/GKV is not just your own premium."

Goal:

- Make the customer recognize a decision trigger.

### Stage 2: Active Research

Customer state:

- Searches Google, YouTube, Reddit, Xiaohongshu, WeChat, Finanzfluss, Finanztip, Feather, CHECK24, Verivox.
- Collects conflicting advice.
- May receive first broker offer.

Questions:

- PKV or GKV?
- Is PKV cheaper or risky?
- What does JAEG mean?
- What happens in retirement?
- Can I go back to GKV?
- Do I need BU?
- What should I disclose?

Surelius role:

- Educator and translator.
- Provide checklists, explainers, decision trees.

Conversion goal:

- Checklist download or offer-upload start.

### Stage 3: Decision Anxiety

Customer state:

- Has enough information to be confused.
- Broker/portal may have provided quotes.
- Customer fears signing the wrong long-term contract.

Emotional state:

- Overwhelmed.
- Suspicious.
- Afraid of irreversibility.
- Embarrassed to not understand.
- Wants someone trustworthy to review.

Surelius role:

- Structured second opinion.
- Recommendation memo.
- Advisor review.

Conversion goal:

- Complete intake.
- Upload offer.
- Book call.

### Stage 4: Advisor Review

Customer state:

- Willing to speak to human, but wary of being sold.

Needs:

- Confirm personal situation.
- Explain trade-offs.
- Compare rejected options.
- Ask "what would you do if you were me?"
- Understand compensation.

Surelius role:

- Human-in-loop decision review.
- No product-specific sales bonus.
- Documented rationale.

Conversion goal:

- Proceed, pause, or reject with confidence.

### Stage 5: Application And Underwriting

Customer state:

- Ready to proceed but still fears mistakes.

Needs:

- Application support.
- Health questions handled carefully.
- Anonymous risk pre-inquiry where needed.
- Timeline/status visibility.

Surelius role:

- Broker execution.
- Underwriting coordination.
- Document tracking.

Conversion goal:

- Successful application or clear fallback.

### Stage 6: Post-Sale Service

Customer state:

- Policy is active.
- Now real-life admin begins.

Needs:

- Claims support.
- Invoice reimbursement help.
- Contract changes.
- Premium increase explanation.
- Annual review.
- Life-event updates.

Surelius role:

- Same advisor team.
- App/portal.
- Claims and contract memory.

Retention goal:

- Customer sees Surelius as ongoing insurance operating system, not one-time sales channel.

## 5. Pain Points By Product

### PKV/GKV

Pain:

- Decision feels irreversible.
- Future contribution risk unclear.
- Family economics are hard.
- Broker incentives are distrusted.
- Tariffs are not comparable by price alone.
- Return-to-GKV rules are confusing.

Customer language:

- "I cannot find relevant decision criteria."
- "Representatives just want to sell."
- "I am afraid of an irreversible decision."
- "I do not understand the tariff differences."

Product response:

- Scenario builder.
- Recommendation memo.
- Rejected-options explanation.
- Family and retirement sensitivity.
- Advisor call.

### BU

Pain:

- Health questions are intimidating.
- Prior therapy/diagnoses create anxiety.
- Risk surcharge/exclusion/rejection is hard to understand.
- Non-Germans may not know why BU is important.

Customer language:

- "What if I cannot get BU?"
- "What should I disclose?"
- "Is anonymous risk pre-inquiry necessary?"

Product response:

- Health-history prep.
- Anonymous risk pre-inquiry.
- Coverage amount guidance.
- Human review before application.

### Life Insurance

Pain:

- People only think about it after family/mortgage trigger.
- Coverage amount is unclear.
- Product confusion between term life and savings-linked products.

Customer language:

- "How much should I insure?"
- "Do I need this if we both work?"
- "Is this for mortgage or family protection?"

Product response:

- Dependency calculator.
- Family/mortgage scenario.
- Term-life education.
- Avoid mixing investment products too early.

### Critical Illness / Dread Disease

Pain:

- Category is less familiar.
- Overlap/confusion with BU and health insurance.
- Customers may not know when it is useful.

Customer language:

- "Is this better than BU?"
- "What exactly counts as critical illness?"

Product response:

- Position as optional layer, not first product.
- Compare with BU, emergency fund, health insurance, income protection.

### Claims And Administration

Pain:

- PKV invoices, reimbursement delays, unclear doctor bills.
- Contract documents scattered.
- Employer benefits/bAV unclear.
- Advisor not reachable after signing.

Product response:

- Policy vault.
- Claims upload and tracking.
- Advisor message center.
- bAV/employer benefits inventory.
- Annual review.

## 6. Objections And Responses

| Objection | What It Really Means | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Are you just another broker?" | Trust problem | Show sample memo, compensation policy, rejected alternatives, advisor quality metrics |
| "Is this free?" | Fear of hidden incentives | Explain compensation plainly before application |
| "Why not CHECK24?" | Comparison vs advice | CHECK24 compares tariffs; Surelius explains whether the decision fits your life scenario |
| "Why not Feather?" | English expat competitor | Feather is strong for English expat onboarding; Surelius focuses on inspectable decision logic and Mandarin/German/English advisor review |
| "Can I just stay in GKV?" | Fear of being sold PKV | Say yes when appropriate; "pause" must be a valid recommendation |
| "I do not want to upload health data." | Privacy and trust | Explain data minimization, consent, storage, deletion, and why certain data matters |
| "I need to ask my spouse." | Shared household decision | Offer shared call and bilingual memo |
| "I already have a broker." | Wants validation, not replacement | Position as second opinion before signing |

## 7. Jobs To Be Done

Functional jobs:

- Compare PKV/GKV.
- Prepare BU application safely.
- Review existing insurance offer.
- Understand long-term cost/risk.
- Submit claims and store policies.

Emotional jobs:

- Feel confident before signing.
- Avoid being manipulated.
- Avoid embarrassing misunderstandings in German.
- Reduce fear of irreversible mistakes.
- Know someone will help later.

Social jobs:

- Explain decision to spouse/family.
- Validate decision with peers.
- Feel responsible and financially mature.
- Avoid being the foreigner who got sold the wrong product.

## 8. Customer Interview Targets

Interview 30 people before scaling:

- 10 Mandarin-speaking high earners.
- 8 English-speaking Indian/international high earners.
- 6 German high earners above JAEG.
- 3 Beamte/Referendare.
- 3 existing PKV customers with claims/admin pain.

Must-have interview questions:

- What triggered your insurance decision?
- Which sources did you check?
- Did you speak to a broker, agent, insurer, comparison portal, or community?
- What did you still not understand after that?
- What made you trust or distrust the advice?
- Did language affect your confidence?
- Would you upload an offer for review?
- Would you pay for a second opinion if no contract is signed?
- What would make you switch from your current broker?
- What post-sale help would matter enough to keep the app?

Validation signal:

- Customer has an active trigger.
- Customer has already spent time researching or speaking to someone.
- Customer can name a specific unresolved risk.
- Customer is willing to share an offer/document.
- Customer wants written rationale, not only a call.

## 10. Customer Success Outcomes

A successful customer should be able to say:

- "I understand why I chose PKV or stayed in GKV."
- "I know what assumptions could change the decision."
- "I know why this BU offer was recommended or rejected."
- "I know how the advisor is compensated."
- "I know who to contact after signing."
- "I can explain the decision to my spouse in our preferred language."

These outcomes should become product metrics, not just marketing claims.
