# Go-To-Market Strategy

## 1. Diagnosis

The opportunity is not "digital insurance is convenient." The sharper problem is:

> High-earning non-native residents in Germany face irreversible, jargon-heavy decisions around PKV, GKV, BU, life insurance, and critical illness cover, but they do not fully trust broker incentives or understand why a recommendation is right for them.

Evidence from prior runs shows repeated public questions around:

- PKV vs GKV uncertainty.
- Broker commission distrust.
- Tariff comparability.
- BU health questions and anonymous risk pre-inquiries.
- Premium shock and old-age PKV cost anxiety.
- People asking Reddit after already speaking to brokers.

The strategy should therefore lead with **trust and decision explanation**, not with "we compare many insurers."

## 2. Target Segments

### Beachhead Segment

Mandarin-speaking and English-speaking high earners in Germany who are:

- Employees above or approaching the JAEG threshold.
- Self-employed professionals with stable income.
- Foreign professionals with Blue Card / skilled-worker profiles.
- Aged roughly 27-45.
- Considering PKV or BU for the first time.
- Not confident enough to sign after reading portals or speaking to a broker.

Estimated immediate Mandarin wedge: 15k-30k high-value prospects in Germany. This is not official market sizing; it is a working funnel from Chinese citizens in Germany to working-age, economically active, likely high-earner residents.

### Expansion Segments

1. Indian high earners in Germany, served primarily in English.
2. German high earners who distrust broker incentives.
3. Russian-speaking professionals, after Mandarin playbook is validated.
4. Families and homeowners needing term life plus BU.

### Do Not Start With

- All expats.
- All Chinese students.
- Low-income newcomers.
- Generic contract-management app users.
- Every insurance line.

Those segments are too broad or have weaker near-term fit for PKV/BU economics.

## 3. Positioning

### Category

Hybrid insurance broker and second-opinion decision platform.

### Positioning Statement

For high-earning non-native residents in Germany who need to decide on PKV, BU, life insurance, or critical illness cover, Surelius is a multilingual hybrid broker that explains the decision logic, documents the recommendation, and keeps a human advisor responsible after the sale. Unlike comparison portals or commission-led sales conversations, Surelius gives customers an inspectable decision memo, advisor review, and ongoing claims support in Mandarin, German, and English.

### Enemy

Opaque insurance sales, tariff confusion, and one-off broker relationships that disappear after contract signing.

### Promise

Understand the decision before you sign, in your language, with a documented recommendation and a human advisor who stays accountable.

### Differentiation

- Mandarin, German, English support from the start.
- Decision memo, not just quote comparison.
- Human review before application.
- Same advisor team after sale.
- Transparent compensation and conflict-management policy.
- App value after the sale, not only acquisition.

## 4. Initial Offer Design

### Offer 1: PKV Second Opinion

For employees above JAEG or self-employed professionals.

Deliverables:

- Digital intake.
- Existing offer upload.
- Short learning path: PKV vs GKV, family scenarios, retirement cost, return-to-GKV rules, tariff stability, doctor invoices.
- Personalized decision memo.
- Red flags and unresolved questions.
- 30-minute advisor review call.
- Optional brokered application.

CTA:

> Upload your PKV offer. Get a Mandarin/German/English second opinion before you sign.

For professionals considering BU.

Deliverables:

- Health-history checklist.
- Explanation of disclosure risk.
- Anonymous risk pre-inquiry preparation.
- Coverage amount guidance.
- Advisor-reviewed application path.

CTA:

For families, mortgage buyers, and couples.

Deliverables:

- BU dependency check.
- Spouse/child PKV scenario.
- "What happens if one income disappears?" walkthrough.

CTA:

> Protect your family plan before buying insurance product by product.

## 5. Trust And Incentive Model

### What To Promise

Use a precise trust promise:

> Our advisors are not paid product-specific sales bonuses. Every recommendation includes a written rationale, rejected alternatives, and compensation disclosure.

Add operational proof:

- Publish a sample recommendation memo.
- Publish a compensation explainer.
- Separate "advisor review quality" from "sales conversion" in internal scorecards.
- Do quarterly recommendation audits.
- Track product mix by advisor and investigate outliers.

### Be Careful With Charity / Commission Rebate Claims

German VAG section 48b restricts special remuneration and commission rebates to policyholders, insured persons, or beneficiaries. A promise to pass "excess brokerage" to the customer or a customer-selected charity may create compliance risk.

Safer alternatives to test with legal counsel:

- Company-level pledge: donate a fixed percentage of annual profits, not tied to individual contracts.
- Product-neutral advisor compensation: advisors get salary plus quality metrics, not product commission.
- Independent audit report on recommendation quality.
- Customer can choose among pre-declared company-supported charities only if counsel confirms this is not a prohibited special benefit.

Do not launch a "we pass your commission to charity" claim before legal review.

## 6. Funnel

### Awareness

Customer trigger:

- Salary crosses PKV threshold.
- Gets PKV offer from broker/portal.
- Receives GKV freiwillig-versichert letter.
- Starts civil-service career.
- Buys property.
- Plans family.
- Has prior therapy/diagnosis and fears BU rejection.

Content:

- Short videos and carousels.
- Reddit/community answers.
- Search landing pages.
- Mandarin WeChat/Xiaohongshu-style posts.

### Consideration

Customer asks:

- "Can I trust my broker?"
- "What questions should I ask before signing?"
- "What happens if I have children?"
- "Will PKV be unaffordable later?"
- "Do I need BU if I have ETFs?"

Assets:

- Offer-review checklist.
- Sample decision memo.
- Scenario calculator.
- Mandarin/German/English glossary.
- "PKV red flags" guide.

### Conversion

Conversion action:

- Upload offer.
- Complete 8-12 minute intake.
- Book advisor review.

Lead qualification:

- Residence in Germany.
- Product interest: PKV, BU, term life, dread disease.
- Income/eligibility.
- Language preference.
- Existing offer or trigger event.

### Activation

Activation moment:

Customer sees a personalized decision memo that explains:

- Why product A is recommended.
- Why product B is rejected.
- Which assumptions matter.
- What an advisor will review.

### Retention

Retention drivers:

- Claims support.
- Invoice reimbursement help.
- Contract overview.
- Annual insurance check.
- Life-event prompts.
- Same advisor team.

## 8. 30/60/90-Day Launch Plan

### First 30 Days

- Launch one landing page for PKV second opinion.
- Create sample decision memo.
- Publish compensation/incentive policy.
- Build German, English, Mandarin intake form.
- Produce 30 short social posts.
- Answer Reddit/community questions manually.
- Interview 10 Mandarin-speaking professionals and 10 English-speaking high earners.

### Days 31-60

- Launch BU health-question prep offer.
- Add offer-upload workflow.
- Create first personalized video prototype manually using Loom or templated video.
- Start monthly Mandarin webinar.
- Build partner list: relocation consultants, tax advisors, immigration lawyers, HR managers, Chinese professional associations.
- Run low-budget retargeting only to site visitors and video engagers.

### Days 61-90

- Add advisor-team handoff process.
- Launch app MVP waitlist around claims, contract vault, and employer benefits overview.
- Publish 2 anonymized case studies.
- Create "PKV decision simulator" beta.
- Test paid search for `PKV second opinion`, `PKV Angebot prüfen`, `德国私保`, `BU Gesundheitsfragen`.
- Decide whether Mandarin wedge converts strongly enough to specialize further.

## 9. Metrics

### Validation Metrics

- 30 qualified calls in 90 days.
- At least 10 Mandarin-language qualified calls.
- 40%+ of qualified leads have existing offer, broker interaction, or live decision trigger.
- 25%+ call-to-paid/revenue-generating conversion once brokerage path is available.
- At least 5 users say the decision memo changed or clarified their decision.

### Channel Metrics

- Reddit: qualified DMs/calls, not upvotes.
- Instagram: saves, shares, DMs, landing-page clicks, qualified call bookings.
- YouTube: watch time on long-form, click-through to offer review, assisted calls.
- Mandarin communities: webinar attendance, WeChat inquiries, booked calls.
- SEO: qualified organic leads, not traffic volume.

### Trust Metrics

- Percentage of recommendations with completed memo.
- Customer-rated clarity after advisor call.
- Advisor response time after sale.
- Claims support satisfaction.
- Product mix concentration by advisor.
- Complaint rate around incentives or pressure.

## 10. Main Risks

1. Mandarin high-earner audience is smaller or harder to reach than expected.
2. Customers like education but do not convert to brokered applications.
3. Compliance limits the strongest trust promises.
4. Advisor capacity becomes bottlenecked.
5. App features distract from the core second-opinion wedge.
6. Incumbents copy multilingual content faster than expected.

## 11. Decision Gate

After 90 days, continue only if:

- At least 30 qualified calls occurred.
- At least 8-10 came from Mandarin or Chinese-community channels.
- At least 10 leads had existing offers or active PKV/BU decisions.
- Users visibly value the memo plus advisor review, not just free advice.

If not, narrow to English-speaking Indian/high-earner expats or German second-opinion users before building a full app.
