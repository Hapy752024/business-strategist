# Consumer-Service Decision Research Synthesis

Researched 2026-07-06 with Europe/Germany and the United States in scope. This topic intentionally uses a dedicated research folder. It supports the `service-customer-perspective-challenger` skill.

## Facts supported by the source set

- The European Commission's 2025 scoreboard reports that online shoppers were more likely than offline shoppers to encounter purchase problems. It also reports widespread concerns about targeted advertising, non-consensual data collection, scams, fake reviews, misleading discounts, and pressure selling.
- The Commission's insurance experiment found that comparison effort and behavioral biases can impair choices. Clear cost display, objective advice, independent and comprehensive comparison, and allowing people to pause and modify selections were identified as useful remedies.
- OECD evidence describes authentic reviews as search and quality aids but documents fake/incentivized reviews, suppressed criticism, non-representativeness, and consumer biases.
- German sources show that reviews and comparison tools are used in online decisions and that consumer-protection bodies warn about portal incentives, fake reviews, misleading service charges, and unwanted contracts.
- US FTC guidance makes review authenticity, material-connection disclosure, honest fee presentation, and cancellation design operational constraints for service marketing.

## Hypotheses derived for service design

- Consequential, unfamiliar, data-sensitive, or hard-to-reverse services should benefit from stronger uncertainty reducers: precise scope, process visibility, credentials, relevant proof, total price, human access, and redress.
- A trigger/job/risk context should predict service choice better than a nationality-based persona.
- Reviews should work better when recent, specific to the service and segment, independently hosted or verifiable, and accompanied by the provider's response to failure—not when only the star average is shown.
- A staged package or paid diagnostic should outperform a large irreversible commitment when outcome variance and expertise asymmetry are high.
- Clear cancellation, refund, data-use, and complaint pathways may improve lead quality even if they reduce superficial conversion.

These are hypotheses until tested on the idea's real segment.

## Judgments encoded in the skill

1. Simulated customer voice is useful for finding blind spots but is not research evidence.
2. National averages must not become personality claims; geography is a contextual layer after trigger, job, risk, price, and category.
3. A customer challenge must produce concrete package, proof, process, and message changes plus behavioral tests.
4. High-risk services require a vulnerable-customer lens, fit boundaries, human escalation, and current jurisdiction-specific compliance review.
5. Marketing success should be judged on qualified commitment and delivery outcomes, not clicks or a persona's stated approval.

## Data gaps

- Limited head-to-head behavioral studies compare the same professional service in Germany, other European countries, and the US.
- Much service-category evidence is concentrated in insurance, healthcare, subscriptions, ecommerce, and platforms.
- German privacy and payment percentages found in vendor surveys require independent replication before use as benchmarks.
- Segment-level differences inside each country can exceed country-average differences.
- The skill must be rerun with direct evidence for the idea's exact category, price, trigger, channel, and buyer.
